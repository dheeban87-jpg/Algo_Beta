Three Levels of Control
Level 1 — Strategic Overseer (Safest, start here)
Claude makes high-level decisions but rules-based system still executes:

Morning: "Today is high-volatility, reduce position sizes by 30%, focus PH5 only"
Midday: "Portfolio is ₹2000 in profit, tighten all stops to breakeven"
EOD: "BAJFINANCE showed strength, add to PH8 watchlist"
Level 2 — Active Decision Maker
Claude approves/rejects every trade before execution:

PH5 finds BAJFINANCE gap → asks Claude → Claude says "YES, 50% size, tight stop"
PH4 TCAS fires → asks Claude → Claude says "EXIT NOW" or "HOLD, momentum reversing"
PH1 scan results → Claude picks the top 2 from 5 candidates
Level 3 — Full Fund Manager (Most powerful, most risky)
Claude decides what to trade, when, and how much:

Receives full market data every 30 seconds
Generates its own trade ideas
Sets its own stops and targets
Manages portfolio-level risk (correlation, sector exposure, drawdown limits)

  Risk Controls (non-negotiable)

  1. Brain directive timeout
     If Claude call fails → last directive stays, max 1 hour
     After 1 hour without response → fallback to NORMAL mode

  2. Hard overrides (human > brain)
     Manual CLOSE always executes regardless of brain directive
     TCAS ALIM always fires — brain cannot block emergency exits

  3. Brain cannot open new positions
     Brain approves/rejects signals that phases FOUND
     Brain never originates "buy X" from scratch (Level 3 gate)
     That stays in Level 3 Phase 2 after paper validation

  4. Directive audit log
     Every brain decision logged: input state + output directive + market outcome
     Weekly review: "did CAUTIOUS days outperform NORMAL days?"
